Brand board · v4 · Rubrication

Oels

Stone, ink and rubric. The company is Oels; the product is Anteroom. Every decision here serves a partner deciding whether this supplier is serious, and a Gulf buyer recognising a visual language that belongs to them.

01 · What changed

Away from cream and brass

Version 3 was limestone and brass. That palette is the default reach for every artisan, craft and heritage brief, and it makes those brands look alike. It also failed on its own terms: brass is unusable as small text on a light ground, which forced a second patch token to work around it.

v4 keeps the heritage and changes the carrier. Deep green stays, because it is Najdi and it is also the green of an Inn of Court. Limestone cools into stone. Brass leaves the accent role entirely and survives only as the lattice thread. The accent becomes rubric.

Why rubric. In Arabic manuscripts and in English legal and monastic manuscripts alike, red ink marked what mattered: the heading, the correction, the clause that binds. The word rubric is ruber, red. Oels sells a system that marks what matters in a conversation and stops when it must, and the record it writes already had a red margin rule down the side of it. The accent was in the product before it was in the palette.

02 · The mark

An aperture standing on the ground

v2 was an arch, because an arch is structurally already the letter A and the company was Anteroom. v4 is an O with an arched head and a flat plinth: still a threshold you see through, now standing on something. The crossbar is rubric, so the mark carries the accent as meaning rather than decoration. It is the line drawn across the record.

03 · Colour

Two grounds, one accent, two lightnesses

The site hard-cuts between ink and stone, and no single accent value clears 4.5:1 on both. The accent is therefore one hue at two lightnesses, keyed to the ground it sits on. One hue for the whole brand; never two.

04 · The motif

The lattice becomes the record

The mashrabiya is the carved screen at a doorway: it filters what passes through and lets the person inside see out without being seen. That is a near-literal description of the product, which is why it earns its place. In v4 it does one more job: it resolves into the ruled lines of an attendance note. Ornament turning into audit trail.

05 · Typography

Caslon and Plex, unchanged

Two families, and they stay. Caslon is 18th-century London and the letterform of legal printing; it carries authority in both markets without costume. Plex handles the disclosure and compliance text that has to stay legible at 13px. Mono is for timestamps, labels and real machine output, never as a costume for “technical”.

Resist any pull toward a decorative Arabic-inspired Latin display face. That is the fastest way to make a heritage brand look like a themed restaurant. If Arabic pages are added, pair with IBM Plex Sans Arabic, which matches the body face exactly.

06 · Actions

One accent, four real states

Rubric is a background behind dark text. It is never light text on a mid accent, which is the failure v3 shipped with. Tab through these: hover, focus and disabled all stay distinct.

The tension worth naming. The buyer today is a UK solicitor or adviser; the Middle East comes later. This palette is built so the heritage reads as quietly architectural to the first audience and as familiar to the second. That dual reading is the whole achievement, and it breaks the moment anything becomes overtly themed. If a decision ever forces a choice between the two, serve the buyer you have now.
