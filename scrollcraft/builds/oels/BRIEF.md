## The feeling curve

Written before the acts. One line per chapter: the emotion, then what causes it.

| # | Chapter | Feeling | What causes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Title page | Composure | Type on stone. No media. The publication announces itself and holds still. |
| I | The front door | Recognition | The lattice resolves into ruled lines. They realise the ornament was a record all along. |
| II | **The call that stops itself** | **Alarm, then relief** | **Entries are accruing. A safeguarding line lands in rubric. The margin rule breaks. Everything stops.** |
| III | What we build | Steadying | After the break, plain substance. Four things, stated flatly. The page is being sober with them on purpose. |
| IV | The keys | Agency | They arm the kill switch themselves and watch the page go quiet. Control transfers to their hand. |
| V | The price | Trust | Numbers on the page, unhidden, where every competitor has a form. |
| VI | Colophon | Resolution | The margin's accumulated marks are revealed as a complete record of what they read. The ask sits in running text. |

No two adjacent chapters share a feeling. Chapter I is quieter than II by
design: the peak needs silence before it.

## The peak

**Chapter II.** The sentence a visitor would say to a colleague:

> "There's a bit where the thing is taking notes, and then it just stops
> because the caller's in trouble, and the red line down the side of the page
> actually breaks."

Largest span on the page. The chapter before it is deliberately quieter.

## Authored silence

Two places, both intentional, so the verification pass does not read them as
dead scroll:

1. **Before the peak.** The last third of Chapter I holds on a nearly empty
   stone ground with one line of type. Roughly 0.5 viewport-heights of
   deliberate quiet.
2. **Inside the peak.** After the margin rule breaks, the stage holds with no
   new content for roughly 0.4 viewport-heights. The silence is the point:
   it is what the caller experiences.

## The tell-someone sentence

> "It's the site where the page keeps a written record of what you just read,
> down the margin, and it breaks when the system stops itself."

## Known tension, recorded deliberately

The client's own website brief says, in its design execution requirements:

> "Motion — deliberate, not scattered ... **No parallax gimmicks, no
> scroll-jacking**"

> "Keep everything around it quiet and disciplined ... **this buyer reads
> over-decoration as unserious**"

The human was shown both quotes and chose "go all out" anyway. Recorded here so
the decision is traceable and reversible, not forgotten. The mitigation is the
grammar: chaptered editorial reads as *a document that behaves*, not as a
consumer showreel, which is the closest a scroll-driven page gets to that brief.
