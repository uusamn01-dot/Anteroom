A model that looks things up in your documents, and shows you where.

Firms tell us they want a model trained on their documents. What they actually want is one that can look things up in them, and cite the source. That is retrieval, it is cheaper, and it updates the moment a document changes.

Boring, well-documented components.

Nothing here is a moat on its own. The moat is that it is configured for one firm and runs where you want it.

Vector store — Qdrant
Self-hostable and fast, with the filtering needed to scope a search to one practice area or date range. pgvector instead if you already run Postgres and want one less service.
Chunking — section aware
Legal documents have structure. We chunk on it rather than every 500 characters, because a clause split in half retrieves badly.
Retrieval — hybrid
Keyword and semantic search together. Pure vector search misses exact section numbers and party names, which is precisely what legal and financial text turns on.
Citation — always
Every answer returns its source. An uncited answer from an internal research tool is worse than useless, because somebody will rely on it.
Embeddings
Hosted for cloud deployments, local open-weight for self-hosted. Changing model later means re-embedding everything, so we decide with you before indexing.

Internal tools for your staff.

Research assistants over your precedent bank, matter triage and classification, document classifiers for incoming post, and a knowledge base over the know-how currently sitting in three partners' heads.

Not client-facing, and we will argue about it if you ask. A retrieval bot answering your clients directly is a different risk profile entirely. Keep it internal until the firm is confident and the logs are boring.
